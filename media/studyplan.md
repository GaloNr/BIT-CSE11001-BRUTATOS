# Suggested study route

For first-year students with no computer science background. About 1 to 1.5 hours per section, including the "Try it yourself" questions.

| Week | Sections | Main question | Check yourself |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1.1, 1.2, 1.3 | What is inside a computer, and how does it store everything as 0s and 1s? | 1.2 Q5 (find the error), 1.3 Q2 (trace a write) |
| 1 | 1.4, 1.5 | How does the CPU run a program, and why are there several kinds of memory? | 1.4 Q1 (trace), 1.5 Q2 (predict) |
| 2 | 1.6, 1.7 | What makes a computer fast, and what hardware does a robot need? | 1.6 Q2 (choose a CPU), 1.7 Q2 (design) |
| 2 | 2.1, 2.2, 2.3 | How do computers find each other and send data in pieces? | 2.2 Q1 (rebuild packets), 2.3 Q1 (valid addresses) |
| 3 | 2.4, 2.5 | Who does which job on the way, and how is data delivered reliably? | 2.5 Q1 (TCP or UDP) |
| 3 | 2.6, 2.7 | What happens when you open a website, and how do you spot a fake one? | 2.6 Q4 (try nslookup), 2.7 Q1 (real or fake) |
| 4 | 3.1, 3.2, 3.3 | Can a machine act smart without thinking? | 3.2 Q1 (thermostat trace), 3.3 Q4 (find the error) |
| 4 | 3.4, 3.5, 3.6 | How do layered behaviors and rewards work, and where do they fail? | 3.4 Q2, 3.5 Q4, 3.6 Q1 |
| 5 | 4.1 | How does one robot vacuum use everything above? | 4.1 Q3 (find the errors) |

Parts 1 and 2 are needed for Part 4. Part 3 can be started after 1.1 and 1.7 if you prefer to begin with AI.
